ROM in Singapore: How to Register Your Marriage

By the VowToDo team · 27 July 2026 · 6 min read

Before the banquet, the gown and the yam seng, there’s one thing that actually makes you married: solemnisation through the Registry of Marriages (ROM). The process is simpler than most couples expect — but it has hard deadlines, so it belongs at the top of your checklist.

The one rule to remember: 21 days

You must file a Notice of Marriage at least 21 days before your solemnisation date. Filing is done online through the Our Marriage Journey portal at marriage.gov.sg using Singpass. Don’t leave it late — popular dates (and popular solemnisers) book out well ahead.

What it costs

SituationFiling fee
At least one of you is a Singapore Citizen or PRS$42
Both of you are foreignersS$380
Changing the date or details after filingS$25

The process, step by step

  • Pick your date and venue. You can solemnise at the ROM building itself, or anywhere else — a restaurant, your banquet venue, a garden, even your living room — with a licensed solemniser.
  • File the Notice of Marriage online and pay the fee.
  • Complete the verification and statutory declaration. Both of you confirm your documents and declare you’re legally free to marry.
  • Confirm your solemniser. If you’re solemnising outside ROM, invite a licensed solemniser and upload their consent. A small appreciation gift (commonly S$80–S$200) is customary.
  • Line up two witnesses aged 21 and above, with valid ID, physically present at the ceremony.
  • Solemnise. The ceremony itself typically takes 15–30 minutes: vows, declarations, and signing the marriage certificate.

Documents to have ready

  • NRIC (citizens/PRs) or passport (foreigners).
  • If previously married: divorce or annulment documents, or a former spouse’s death certificate.
  • Deed poll if you’ve changed your name.
  • Parental consent if either of you is 18–20 years old.

ROM first, banquet later?

Plenty of Singapore couples split the two: a small ROM solemnisation first (often at an intimate venue with immediate family), then the customary wedding and banquet months or even a year later. There’s no rule against it — just decide early, because it changes your whole planning timeline, from the gown to the guest list.

Muslim marriages are registered separately through the Registry of Muslim Marriages (ROMM), with its own requirements — see marriage.gov.sg.
