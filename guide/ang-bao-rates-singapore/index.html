Ang Bao Rates: How Much to Give at a Singapore Wedding

VowToDo · 27 July 2026 · 6 min

The working rule at a Singapore wedding: your ang bao should cover your seat. Venues price that seat very differently, so the amount depends on where the couple is hosting you, and whether it is lunch or dinner.

Market rates in 2026, per person

Where you are goingTypical range
Luxury hotel dinnerS$250 and up
Mid-tier hotel dinnerS$170–250
Restaurant banquetS$100–170
Casual venues: clubs, halls, a void deckS$60–100

Lunch usually sits under dinner. A weekday sits under a weekend. If you know the venue, look up the current price per table, divide by ten, and round up. That is a better guide than a number you remember from a cousin’s wedding in 2019.

The etiquette around the number

  • Closeness adds. The rate covers the seat. If you are close to the couple, add something on top. That part is the gift.
  • Even, and avoid 4. Even amounts read as more auspicious. Many people land on an 8: S$188, S$288.
  • A pair is one ang bao for two seats.
  • Not attending. A smaller token, commonly S$50 to S$100, still marks the day.
  • Bring it that night. There is a reception desk, a box, and usually someone trusted who does not leave the box.

When it is not a Chinese banquet

  • Malay weddings. Around S$50 to S$100 a person is a warm norm at a kenduri.
  • Indian weddings. S$50 to S$80 is common. Adding a S$1 coin, so the total is odd, is a small auspicious habit.
  • Church, then food. A ceremony with a light reception: S$50 or more is gracious. If a full banquet follows, use banquet rates.
These ranges are mid-2026, and they drift up with banquet prices. For a venue you do not know, the current per-table price is the accurate guide.
