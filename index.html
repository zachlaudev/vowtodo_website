01 · Inside

The jobs a Singapore wedding actually throws at you.

A legal filing, a family morning, and a dinner priced by the table. The app is shaped around that, not around a generic Western timeline.

Checklist

Due when it is due.

Tasks sit in groups: twelve months out, then six, then the week of. The ROM notice belongs 21 days before solemnisation. If you solemnise months before the banquet, those are two different dates, and the list knows the difference.

Reminders go to the person who owns the task. You stop being the person who chases.

Budget

One total, in dollars.

A hotel quotes S$1,500 a table and means about S$1,800 after service charge and GST. Twenty tables is the banquet. Photography, the gown, Guo Da Li and the solemniser sit in the same roll-up, next to what you have already paid.

The couple sees the money. Crew sees their jobs, not your balance, unless you open it.

02 · Compared

A spreadsheet stores names. It does not collect the RSVPs.

A spreadsheet

  • You build every tab, and you maintain it.
  • Anyone with the link can edit the budget.
  • It never reminds the person who owns the task.
  • RSVPs arrive in chat, then you type them in.
  • Seating is a grid of names and a prayer.
  • Free, and also a second job.

VowToDo

  • Checklist through seating, already there.
  • Couple, admins, and crew. Different access.
  • A due date nudges the person it belongs to.
  • One link. Answers file themselves.
  • Drag a guest onto a seat. Swap when you must.
  • Free. The whole wedding. Helpers too.

Plenty of wedding tools are free because the next screen is an ad, a vendor marketplace, or a locked guest. VowToDo is only the planner.
