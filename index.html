The shape of the day

Paperwork, a ceremony, and a party. Often three different days.

VowToDo is where they stay written down, instead of living in five family chats and a spreadsheet nobody wants to open.

  • The paperworkA licence, a notice, a registrar. It has a deadline, and that deadline is often not the party.
  • The moneyPlanned, paid, and still to come, in whatever currency you use. The couple sees it. Helpers do not, unless you open it.
  • The peopleOne list. A link for replies. A room you can actually seat.
  • The dayTimes, and the name of the person holding each one. Someone on the gifts. Someone on the grandparents.

Checklist

Due when it is actually due.

Tasks sit in the month they belong to. The marriage licence is not buried in “the week of the party.” If the legal wedding and the celebration are months apart, those are two dates, and the list can tell them apart.

One total, in dollars.

Traditions

Sweet tea, in the right order.

Grandparents first, then parents, then the uncles and aunts. Someone else holds the pot. The couple should not be reading kinship titles off a phone in the moment, so the order lives in the run sheet.

The ceremony

A garden, a chapel, a registry office.

The ceremony is whatever makes it real for you. A short civil signing, a long service, vows in a garden. Guests reply from a link in any browser. The seating chart is the part you will want on a laptop, when someone has to move.
