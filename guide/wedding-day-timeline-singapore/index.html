The Wedding Day, Hour by Hour

By the VowToDo team · 27 July 2026 · 7 min read

A Singapore Chinese wedding is really two events in one day: a customs-filled morning across two homes, and a formal evening banquet. The couples who enjoy their day are the ones who padded the schedule and delegated ruthlessly. Here’s a realistic template for a dinner-banquet wedding.

The morning

TimeWhat’s happening
5:30amBride’s makeup and hair begin; photographer/videographer arrive at the bride’s home.
7:30amGroom and brothers set off in the bridal car (decorated, with a “toll” ang bao ready).
8:00amGatecrashing — door games run by the sisters. Budget 45–60 minutes; it always runs long.
9:00amGroom “finds” the bride; veil lifted, vows or a quiet moment; family photos.
9:30amTea ceremony at the bride’s home (order varies — some families do groom’s side first).
10:30amTravel to the groom’s home; bride formally welcomed.
11:00amTea ceremony at the groom’s home, then a simple family lunch.
1:00pmRest. Protect this block — you’ve been up since 5.

The evening banquet

TimeWhat’s happening
5:00pmCouple arrives at the venue; sound check, run-through with emcee and banquet manager.
6:30pmCocktail reception; guests arrive, sign in, ang bao box guarded; photo montage plays.
7:30pmDoors open, guests seated. First march-in; first dish served.
8:30pmSpeeches and toasts between courses; highlights video from the morning if your videographer offers same-day-edit.
9:15pmSecond march-in (outfit change), cake-cutting, and the yam seng — three rounds.
9:45pmTable-to-table toasts and photos.
10:30pmSend-off line at the door; count the ang bao box with two trusted people before leaving.

Helper roles to assign in advance

  • Best man / brothers: transport, gatecrash negotiation, groom’s bag.
  • Sisters: door games, tea-ceremony logistics, bride’s kit (heels, safety pins, lipstick).
  • Ang bao guardians: two trusted people on the reception desk, never leaving the box unattended.
  • Emcee(s): a friend or a pro; give them the run sheet and name pronunciations days early.
  • Family wrangler: one person per side who knows every uncle and can herd them for photos.
  • Vendor point-of-contact: someone who is not you fielding calls on the day.

Three rules that save every timeline

  • Add 15-minute buffers between blocks — the gatecrash and tea ceremonies always overrun.
  • Feed the couple. Someone should be assigned to make sure you actually eat.
  • Share the final schedule with everyone involved, not just the wedding party.
